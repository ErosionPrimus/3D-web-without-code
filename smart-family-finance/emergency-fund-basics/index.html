Emergency Fund Basics: What It Is, How Much You Need, Where to Keep It

By FocusedBuzz Editorial · 7 min read

An emergency fund is a financial buffer for unexpected expenses — car repairs, medical bills, job loss. Without it, any surprise becomes a debt spiral.

How much?

  • Minimum: $1,000 (covers most minor emergencies)
  • Standard: 3 months of essential expenses
  • Robust: 6 months of essential expenses
  • Freelancers/commission workers: 6–9 months

Where to keep it

  • High-yield savings account (4–5% APY in 2026) — accessible, insured, earns interest
  • Money market fund — slightly higher yield, same-day access
  • Avoid: CDs (penalty for early withdrawal), stocks (volatile), under-mattress (no interest, no insurance)

Building it

  1. Start with $500 — even $25/week reaches that in 20 weeks
  2. Automate transfers on payday — "pay yourself first"
  3. Use windfalls (tax refunds, bonuses) to accelerate
  4. Once funded, redirect savings to retirement or debt payoff

When to use it

True emergencies only: medical, car repair, essential home repair, job loss. Not a sale, not a vacation, not a new phone.

Bottom line

$1,000 eliminates most financial shocks. 3 months of expenses provides real security. See our annual checkup to prioritize.
