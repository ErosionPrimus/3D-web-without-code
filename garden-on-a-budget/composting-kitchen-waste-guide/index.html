Composting Kitchen Waste: A Beginner's Guide to Free Fertilizer

By FocusedBuzz Editorial · 7 min read

Composting turns kitchen scraps into nutrient-rich soil amendment — for free. Here is how to start.

What to compost

  • Greens (nitrogen): Fruit/vegetable scraps, coffee grounds, eggshells, fresh grass clippings
  • Browns (carbon): Dry leaves, cardboard, newspaper, straw, wood chips
  • Avoid: Meat, dairy, oils, diseased plants, pet waste

The ratio

3 parts brown to 1 part green. Too green = smelly. Too brown = slow decomposition.

Methods

  • Pile: Simple, free, 3–6 months
  • Bin: Tumbler or stationary, neater, faster
  • Vermicompost: Worms in a bin — indoor, fast, premium castings

Maintenance

  • Turn weekly for aeration
  • Keep moist like a wrung-out sponge
  • Chop scraps small for faster breakdown

When it's ready

Dark, crumbly, earthy smell. 3–6 months for basic pile, 1–2 months for tumbler/vermicompost.

Bottom line

Zero cost, high reward. See our soil building guide for using compost.
