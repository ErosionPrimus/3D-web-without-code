Starting a Vegetable Garden on a Budget: First-Year Survival Guide

By FocusedBuzz Editorial · 10 min read

A vegetable garden can save $500–$1,000/year in grocery bills — but only if you start smart. Here is the budget-conscious approach.

Start small

A 4×8 ft raised bed or 3–5 containers is enough for year-one. Expand only after you succeed.

Cheap starts

  • Seeds: $2–$3/packet, 50–100 seeds each
  • Seedlings: $2–$4 at farmers markets vs. $6–$8 at garden centers
  • Cuttings: Many vegetables (tomatoes, basil) root from cuttings in water

Free soil

See our soil building guide — compost, leaf mold, cover crops.

What to grow for ROI

  • Tomatoes ($5 retail vs. $0.50 seed)
  • Herbs (basil, cilantro — $3/bunch at store)
  • Lettuce/greens (continuous harvest)
  • Peppers, zucchini, beans

Common first-year mistakes

  • Too big — leads to burnout
  • Wrong sun exposure — most veggies need 6+ hours
  • Overwatering — kills more plants than underwatering
  • Ignoring pests — check plants daily, early intervention

Bottom line

First-year cost: $30–$60. Potential savings: $500+. For seasonal planning, see our calendar guide.
